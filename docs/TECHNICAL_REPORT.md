# MetroSched – Technical Report

> **Synthetic data notice.** All results below come from simulation runs on synthetic
> scenarios generated by this project. They are not KMRL data and have not been
> validated against KMRL operations.

## 1. Problem

Each operating day the depot must release ("induct") trains onto the mainline so that
enough trains are in service to carry the passenger demand, while respecting hard
constraints: one train per track block and platform, minimum safety headways, temporary
closures, train readiness (preparation done, no pending maintenance), induction windows and
a rolling-hour induction cap. Inducting too late leaves passengers without service;
inducting too early or in bunches creates queues on the single transfer line, wastes
train-hours and can exhaust induction capacity before the evening peak.

## 2. Simulation (Phase 1)

See [`PHASE1_DESIGN.md`](PHASE1_DESIGN.md). In brief: SimPy model of one day (04:00 depot open,
05:00–23:00 service), 25 trainsets, 2 depot exit tracks → transfer line → depot junction →
UP/DN mainline entry junctions → 2 platforms. Running trains pass the entry junctions every
half-cycle, so mainline traffic competes with inductions. Three scenario types (normal,
peak-demand, disruption) vary demand, preparation overruns, faults, closures, headways and
capacity. Failure probability in service grows with km since maintenance, so *which* train
is inducted matters.

**Fair comparison.** All schedulers use the same external-control interface: every 60 s
they may request up to 3 inductions. Each request goes through
`MetroEnvironment.apply_induction`, which atomically checks readiness and capacity and then
lets the route enforce block occupancy, headway and closures. Each train has its own random
stream (common random numbers), so all schedulers face the same faults, delays and failures
on a given seed.

## 3. Schedulers

**FCFS baseline** (`scheduling/baseline_fcfs.py`): when committed trains < trains required
*now*, induct the earliest-ready train; the environment assigns the earliest-clearing exit
track and entry point. No look-ahead, no congestion awareness.

**Rule-based heuristic** (`scheduling/heuristic_scheduler.py`): target = max requirement
over the next 12 min; hold if a train is already queued on the induction route (unless
service is short now); choose the best induction priority (lowest km since maintenance);
choose the entry point that clears soonest at the train's arrival.

**PPO agent** (`reinforcement_learning/`, `scheduling/rl_scheduler.py`).

* *Observation* (78 values): time of day; required trains now, +10, +20, +30, +60 min;
  demand now and +30 min; trains in service, committed, being inducted, queued,
  withdrawing, delayed; current and 30-min-ahead service gap; remaining induction capacity
  (rolling hour, window); inductions in the last hour; time since last induction; ready /
  pending / maintenance counts; for each of 7 track sections occupancy, time to clear,
  closure, queue; and for 6 candidate trains presence, priority, km since maintenance,
  time since ready.
* *Action* `Discrete(13)`: 0 = wait; 1 + 2k + e = induct candidate k (3 best-priority + 3
  earliest-ready trains) via entry point e (UP/DN). This covers "which train", "which slot"
  and "wait for a better opportunity".
* *Invalid actions* are rejected by the simulation and cannot move a train. During training
  the infeasible set is masked (`MaskablePPO`, sb3-contrib); at deployment the same mask is
  applied as a safety shield. Hard safety never depends on the policy.
* *Training*: behaviour-cloning warm start on heuristic demonstrations (training seeds
  only), then masked PPO on the multi-objective reward; the model with the best return on
  separate validation seeds is kept. Plain PPO from scratch was tried first and did not
  learn within the CPU budget (see §5).

## 4. Reward function

Computed every 60 s simulated (`reinforcement_learning/reward_function.py`); rates are per
train-minute, events per occurrence.

| Term | Weight | Sign | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Service shortfall (required − in service) | 0.30 | − | Primary objective: passengers without capacity. Largest rate weight. |
| Missed induction opportunity (demand slot expires unfilled) | 1.00 | − | A whole slot of demand never served; roughly 3 train-minutes of shortfall. |
| Useful induction (fills a gap now / within 10 min) | 0.50 | + | Positive signal for successful, timely inductions; small vs shortfall so inducting is never rewarded for its own sake. |
| In-service count within [required, required+1] | 0.05 | + | Keeps the service headway close to the demand-derived target. |
| Unnecessary deployment (deployed − need₃₀ₘᵢₙ − 1) | 0.03 | − | Wasted train-hours / energy; 10× smaller than shortfall because under-supply hurts passengers more than over-supply. |
| Trains queued on the induction route | 0.05 | − | Waiting time; bunched dispatches block the single transfer line. |
| New queueing event (track contention) | 0.10 | − | Discourages creating conflicts that the safety logic must resolve. |
| Delayed trains in service | 0.02 | − | Operational delays; weak, because mostly exogenous. |
| In-service failure | 1.00 | − | Penalises inducting worn (high-km) trains. |
| Rejected action | 0.10 | − | Constraint-violating requests (only reachable with plain PPO; masked during MaskablePPO training). |

Weight ordering follows the operating priorities: serve demand first, then avoid
missed slots and failures, then waiting and contention, then waste. The weights are in
`data/config/rl_config.yaml`; `tests/test_rl_environment.py` checks signs and ratios. Safety
constraints are **not** in the reward as a substitute for enforcement; they are enforced by
the environment.

## 5. Training and results

### 5.1 Training procedure (what was actually run)

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Algorithm | MaskablePPO (sb3-contrib), MLP 128-128, γ = 0.995, λ = 0.95, lr 3·10⁻⁵, clip 0.1, 5 epochs, target-KL 0.015, n_steps 2048 × 2 envs |
| Warm start | behaviour cloning on 60 heuristic episodes (68 596 decisions, training seeds), 40 epochs; action accuracy 96.9 %; critic pre-fitted on discounted returns |
| PPO steps | 400 000 (≈ 350 training days), 31.8 min on a 2-core CPU |
| Training scenarios | seeds drawn from [0, 10⁶); mix normal 45 % / peak 30 % / disruption 25 % |
| Model selection | best mean return on 12 fixed **validation** days (seeds 500 000 000+), evaluated every 20 000 steps |
| Action selection | masked policy, sampled with an RNG seeded by the scenario seed (reproducible) |
| Checkpoints | `models/checkpoints/` every 50 000 steps; `ppo_metrosched_bc_init`, `_best`, `_final`, selected `ppo_metrosched.zip` |

Convergence (`results/plots/06_reward_convergence.png`, `results/training/validation_curve.csv`):
the validation return of the selected model was **12.7**, against **−27.3** for FCFS and
**+17.3** for the heuristic on the same 12 validation days. During PPO fine-tuning the
validation return stayed between 8.2 and 12.6, so under the selection rule the step-0
(warm-started) policy was kept. Stochastic training returns (rolling mean ≈ 15–30) are
higher than validation returns because validation over-weights disruption days (1/3 vs 1/4).

**What did not work (kept for transparency, `results/archive/run1/`).**
1. *Plain PPO from a random policy* (400k-step budget): validation return stayed ≈ −460,
   because 12 of 13 actions are "induct" at initialisation and the cost of over-inducting
   (window capacity exhausted hours later) is a very delayed signal. Masking alone
   (60k-step test) did not change this.
2. *Run 1* (warm start + PPO, lr 10⁻⁴, deterministic **argmax** validation): validation was
   unstable (−92 … −6). On the 12 validation days the same run-1 model scored **−4.5 with
   argmax vs +12.4 with seeded sampling**. After imitation, WAIT is the single most
   probable action at almost every tick, so argmax delays inductions. Run 2 (reported here)
   therefore validates and deploys with seeded sampling and uses more conservative PPO
   updates. The reward weights were not changed between runs.

### 5.2 Evaluation protocol

* 120 **unseen** days: generator seeds 900 000 000 … 900 000 119, 40 per scenario type,
  disjoint from training seeds [0, 10⁶) and validation seeds (checked in code).
* Every scheduler runs every day under identical conditions: same demand, fleet,
  closures, depot faults and per-train random streams.
* Metrics are computed from the simulation logs (`evaluation/metrics.py`); improvement
  = (FCFS − X)/FCFS × 100 for lower-is-better metrics and (X − FCFS)/FCFS × 100 for
  higher-is-better metrics. 95 % CIs, paired bootstrap CIs and exact sign tests are in
  `results/evaluation/summary.csv`.

Per scenario (`results/plots/09_scenario_comparison.png`): the gains over FCFS hold on
normal, peak-demand and disruption days. Disruption days are hardest for every scheduler;
there, average induction delay is 7.8 min (FCFS), 3.1 (heuristic) and 3.1 (PPO).

### 5.4 Interpretation

* **Against the FCFS baseline**, the PPO agent improves every service metric:
  induction delay −75 %, shortfall −70 %, missed opportunities −43 %, waiting time −16 %,
  coverage 98.7 → 99.6 %. All are significant under a paired sign test. The main mechanism is
  anticipation: it inducts before demand rises instead of reacting to a shortfall.
* **Costs.** PPO deploys slightly more train-hours (+7 %) and causes slightly more
  queueing events (+7 %). It inducts earlier and somewhat more often (24.1 vs 22.6 per
  day).
* **Against the rule-based heuristic**, PPO is **not** better overall. It has the lowest
  waiting time of the three (−3.9 % vs heuristic, p = 0.02). The heuristic is better on
  induction delay (1.21 vs 1.45 min), shortfall (0.65 vs 0.93 train-h) and missed
  opportunities (0.23 vs 0.38). Within this compute budget, PPO fine-tuning did not exceed
  its imitation warm start on the validation days. A stronger agent would need more
  training (multi-core, millions of steps) or richer features (e.g. explicit window-
  capacity forecasts). The heuristic is therefore a strong, recommended fallback.
* **Supplementary check** (`results/evaluation/supplementary_ppo_final_400k/`): the
  fully fine-tuned 400k-step policy, evaluated afterwards on the same 120 days, scored
  somewhat better than the selected model (waiting 3.33 min, conflicts 11.3, missed 0.32,
  shortfall 0.80 train-h). Selecting it *because* of these test results would be test-set
  selection, so the pre-registered validation-based choice remains the reported model.
  The 12 validation days were too noisy (std ≈ 10 reward units) to separate the two.
* **Safety:** 0 hard violations in all 360 evaluated days, 0 rejected actions; safety does
  not depend on the learned policy.

## 6. Limitations

* Synthetic, single-line abstraction; parameters are illustrative, not KMRL's.
* The trained policy is tied to the infrastructure layout it was trained on (number of
  track sections → observation size). The dashboard refuses PPO for other layouts.
* Demand is exogenous and does not react to service quality; crew rostering, energy and
  stabling order inside the depot are not modelled.
* Validation used 12 days. More validation days would make model selection more reliable.
